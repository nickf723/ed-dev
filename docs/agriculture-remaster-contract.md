# Agriculture remaster contract

## Scope

This checkpoint consolidates the already-strong Agriculture hub. It preserves the whole-farm transect and nitrogen-pathway instrument while moving their hierarchy, arithmetic, vocabulary, evidence review, data boundaries, and background into deterministic shared contracts.

## Ontology

- Agriculture keeps nine direct branches: Agronomy & Crop Science; Soil Science & Nutrient Management; Horticulture; Animal Science & Livestock Systems; Aquaculture; Forestry & Agroforestry; Agroecology & Sustainable Agriculture; Agricultural Engineering & Technology; and Agricultural Economics & Food Systems.
- The production/resources banks are visual groupings only. They do not create false curriculum parents or imply that ecology, technology, soils, or economics sit outside production.
- All nine branches remain visible and inert until their own pages exist.
- The root owns whole-farm systems, management, inputs and outputs, tradeoffs, risk, resilience, stewardship, harvest, residues, season, field scale, food systems, and the farm-gate boundary. Specialty terminology belongs to its narrowest current branch.

## Learning flow

The root proceeds from direct branch navigation through a whole-farm system stack, a normalized nitrogen ledger, six cross-system questions, a four-file evidence assessment, and a public-data protocol. The assessment combines exact arithmetic with transfer distinctions about residue timing, aggregate agricultural estimates, and mapped soil evidence.

## Models and boundaries

- The canonical nitrogen fixture starts with 45 mineral units, adds 24 external units, applies a 13.75% modeled loss fraction, and leaves 59.5125 units before plant uptake.
- Biological fixation is capped by remaining plant demand and is not represented as a universal soil credit.
- Retained residue enters an organic pathway; it does not instantly reappear in the current mineral pool.
- The model is normalized bookkeeping, not a fertilizer rate, yield forecast, nutrient recommendation, or representation of every nitrogen transformation.
- County estimates, soil map units, remote sensing, prices, and on-field observations have different populations, scales, methods, update schedules, and decision uses.

## Vocabulary

Thirty-six terms have one narrowest current owner: eighteen at Agriculture and two within each of its nine direct branches. Parent drawers aggregate the terms through curriculum containment, allowing future branch lessons to extend rather than copy the vocabulary tree.

## Visual and hydration contract

The whole-farm identity remains isometric and spatially integrated: distinct fields, hedgerows, orchard, pasture, protected culture, pond, lane, irrigation, buildings, a soil profile, roots, and nutrient cues occupy one deterministic server-rendered SVG. The removed canvas may not return through viewport reads, animation frames, device-pixel measurements, document visibility state, or hydration-only scenery. Client state remains limited to the nitrogen and assessment islands.

## Data and repository boundary

The page links official USDA NASS developer guidance, NRCS Web Soil Survey, NRCS Soil Data Access, and USDA ERS data products. A future field atlas must retain provider, item or statistic identity, commodity or soil component, unit, geography, reference period, release or refresh date, methodology, suppression, revision, query, and stable source. NASS Quick Stats requires a key-aware server adapter; Soil Data Access requires bounded spatial and query contracts. The root performs no render-time provider fetch.

## Verification

The checkpoint must pass targeted React/Next lint, TypeScript, deterministic model tests, vocabulary aggregation, the three informational audits, a production build, generated-HTML checks for branch parity, the initial nitrogen state, sources, assessment, metadata, and static background, plus a zero-deployment guard before publication to `studio`. Responsive rendering and interaction checks remain queued when the browser runner is unavailable.
