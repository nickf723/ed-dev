# Religion remaster contract

## Educational job

The Religion root is an eight-field navigation hub and source-interpretation
lesson. It helps a learner choose among methods, communities, texts, ritual,
material life, society, theology/philosophy, and sacred narrative; examine one
fictional fieldnote through multiple lenses; distinguish descriptive academic
claims from confessional ones; preserve internal diversity; calculate one
bounded archive share; and keep collection access separate from interpretive or
reuse permission.

It is not a ranking of religions, a checklist of universal beliefs, sacred
geometry, a theology course presented as neutral comparison, a myth-equals-
false-story glossary, or a substitute for the eight direct fields.

## Registry and navigation contract

`RELIGION_CURRICULUM` owns exactly eight direct fields in this order:

1. Religious Studies & Methods — planned
2. Religious Traditions & Communities — planned
3. Sacred Texts & Interpretation — planned
4. Ritual, Practice & Experience — planned
5. Material Religion, Art & Place — planned
6. Religion, Society & Politics — planned
7. Theology & Philosophy of Religion — planned
8. Mythology & Sacred Narrative — active

The page derives labels, descriptions, statuses, hrefs, and breadcrumbs from
the registry and fails loudly when model order drifts. The active route links;
planned routes remain visible and inert. The source spine, fieldnote instrument,
study boundaries, evidence review, and source shelf are reference or lesson
content rather than hidden navigation.

## Lesson flow

1. Eight-field route atlas and claim-making spine
2. Six-lens fictional fieldnote instrument
3. Religious-studies/theology/tradition/comparison boundaries
4. Four-dossier evidence review
5. Professional-practice, archive, and object-collection boundaries

Major sections use 96px scenery corridors so the comparative reading table can
remain visible between foreground surfaces.

## Shared model and exact practice

`religionModel.ts` owns direct branch parity, bounded source-share arithmetic,
deterministic percent formatting, and four evidence answers.

The exact practice fixture is:

`9 packet records mentioning a procession / 24 packet records = 37.5%`

That result describes the curated packet, not all practitioners, events,
places, periods, motives, or meanings. The other dossiers test text and
reception, dimension-specific comparison, and collection-access boundaries.

## Vocabulary ownership

Religion owns 10 broad terms. Each direct field owns two specialized terms, for
26 unique terms in the subtree. Stable legacy IDs for theodicy, eschatology, and
syncretism remain unique but move to Theology & Philosophy of Religion or
Religion, Society & Politics. Religion scopes aggregate automatically into the
Humanities drawer with curriculum-node provenance.

The older global mythology vocabulary remains separate until Greek and wider
mythology pages receive their own bounded ownership audit; it is not silently
folded into this root.

## Visual and hydration contract

The root background is a deterministic server-rendered SVG comparative reading
table. Archive fragments and oral/material records feed seven analytical lens
points around one bounded claim, then remain visible beside a provisional
interpretive dossier. It uses no canvas, viewport reads, random values, timers,
animation frame, or hydration-time drawing.

The preserved fieldnote instrument initializes to Ritual & Body and changes
through native buttons. The evidence review initializes from fixed model data.
Required interface copy stays at least 11px, focus is visible, and motion is not
necessary to understand the route.

## Ethics and repository boundaries

The page links to official sources and performs no render-time fetch:

- [AAR responsible research practices](https://aarweb.org/news/responsible-research-practices/)
  for methodological transparency, fair treatment, accountability, human-
  subject responsibility, and the special authority communities may attribute
  to religious life;
- [Library of Congress JSON/YAML API](https://www.loc.gov/apis/json-and-yaml/)
  for faceted collection, item, resource, image, and bibliographic records;
- [The Met Collection API](https://metmuseum.github.io/) for object metadata and
  rights-aware public-domain media.

A future adapter must retain provider and collection, stable item/resource or
object ID, title, contributors or culture fields as provider assertions, date,
place, format/classification, description, credit, rights/access/media state,
traditional-knowledge labels when supplied, source URL, pagination, and refresh
context. It must support sensitive, restricted, incomplete, and no-media states.
Searchability, public metadata, or CC0 catalog data is not proof of theological
authority, community endorsement, complete context, legal ownership, cultural
permission, or unrestricted media reuse.
