# Economics Remaster Contract

## Identity and rotation role

Economics is a Social Science navigation hub and an introductory lesson about constrained choice, exchange, institutions, aggregate accounting, measurement, and causal evidence. It follows Materials Science to change academic world, visual grammar, model language, and assessment style. The page should feel like a connected economy and a claim ledger: flows move among households, firms, government, and the rest of the world, while every conclusion is reconciled to the reasoning system that produced it.

The page must not reduce economics to finance, money, stock prices, or personal wealth; present equilibrium as fair or optimal; treat GDP as wellbeing; treat one price change as inflation; or present association as a causal policy estimate.

## Bounded direct-child ontology

Economics owns seven direct branches:

1. Microeconomics
2. Macroeconomics
3. Econometrics
4. Behavioral Economics
5. International Economics
6. Public Economics
7. Development Economics

The branch atlas presents Microeconomics and Macroeconomics as different scales of inquiry, Econometrics as a measurement toolkit used across scales, and four applied fields as cross-cutting problem areas. These are coordinated entry points rather than an exhaustive partition. Finance, labor, health, environment, industrial organization, economic history, and narrower specialties belong inside an existing branch until a substantive ontology review establishes a new peer.

All seven current branches are planned and non-clickable until substantive pages exist.

## Overview lesson flow

The root follows an Orient → Navigate → Relate → Model → Check sequence:

1. orient around scarcity, tradeoffs, marginal reasoning, and institutions;
2. navigate all seven direct branches before extended lesson content;
3. relate household, firm, government, and international flows at micro, macro, and policy scales;
4. manipulate one normalized linear supply–demand model while stating what is held fixed;
5. assess an accounting identity, a comparative-static result, a price measure, and a causal claim.

The root does not attempt to survey every economic school, provide investment advice, forecast a real market, estimate a policy effect, or make a complete welfare judgment.

## Deterministic model contract

One shared module owns exact branch parity, the normalized market equations, baseline equilibrium, shift interpretation, expenditure-GDP arithmetic, and assessment fixtures.

The market instrument uses local teaching equations:

- demand: `P = 90 + 10d - Q`;
- supply: `P = 10 - 10s + Q`;
- equilibrium: the price and quantity that satisfy both relationships.

`d` and `s` range from −2 through 2. The baseline is `P = 50`, `Q = 40`. When both curves shift right one step, the symmetric fixture yields `P = 50`, `Q = 50`. These are normalized indices with no currency, quantity unit, empirical calibration, uncertainty, adjustment path, market power, strategic response, or distributional information.

The server-rendered background shows static exchange streams, institutional nodes, and an accounting ledger without random values, viewport measurement, canvas, animation ownership, or a client-only first frame. The resource-flow topology keeps selection and scale controls but uses stable path markers rather than SVG motion loops.

## Assessment contract

The claim ledger contains four deterministic files:

- calculate `500 + 120 + 160 + 90 − 110 = 760` using the expenditure approach to GDP;
- calculate the page model's simultaneous one-step rightward demand and supply shifts as `P = 50`, `Q = 50` without turning the result into a universal forecast;
- identify an 18% gasoline-price rise as one price observation rather than a complete inflation measure;
- treat cross-regional minimum-wage and employment co-variation as an association requiring a credible causal design.

Every file supports unanswered, correct, incorrect, changed-answer, switched-file, and reset states. Feedback reconstructs the accounting, model, measurement, or causal boundary rather than merely announcing a score.

## Vocabulary ownership

Economics owns twenty root terms: scarcity, opportunity cost, marginal analysis, incentive, institution, market, supply, demand, equilibrium, shortage, surplus, elasticity, externality, public good, GDP, inflation, price index, real value, productivity, and causal effect.

The terms register at `social.economics` and aggregate into the Social Science drawer through curriculum containment. As child pages are built, narrower terms should move to their owning child registrations while remaining inherited by this parent.

## Source and model boundary

The GDP identity and domestic-production boundary are checked against the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis. The representative-basket and price-index boundary is checked against the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI overview.

The flow topology, linear market, GDP ledger, basket, and cross-regional comparison are local schematic teaching fixtures. They are not empirical records, official estimates, forecasts, policy evaluations, financial guidance, or welfare rankings.
