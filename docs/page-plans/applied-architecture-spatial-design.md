# Spatial Design & Program Unit Plan

## Identity

**Page:** Spatial Design & Program  
**Route:** `/applied-science/architecture/spatial-design`  
**Parent:** Architecture  
**Page type:** Unit

## Unit throughline

**activities → required spaces → adjacency → circulation → human scale**

The unit teaches how a human brief becomes spatial logic before a floor plan becomes fixed geometry.

## Learning contract

A learner should understand that spatial design begins with people, activities, relationships, movement, and dimensional needs rather than drawing walls first.

## Child lessons

1. **Program & Area** — what spaces are needed and approximately how much area must they support?
2. **Adjacency & Zoning** — which spaces should be near, flexible, separated, public, shared, or staff-oriented?
3. **Circulation & Wayfinding** — how do people enter, move, choose directions, queue, and find destinations?
4. **Human Scale & Accessibility** — how do body dimensions, furniture, clearances, reach, and inclusive access affect spatial size and arrangement?

## Unit-page responsibility

The unit page should orient and sequence, not reproduce four full instruments. Its signature model is a compact brief → spaces → adjacency → circulation progression using one simple library example.

## Accuracy constraints

- Do not imply that a spatial program determines one correct plan.
- Keep accessibility relevant across the entire unit even though one lesson studies it directly.
- Do not turn circulation into a decorative arrow overlay; later lessons must model actual movement and decision points.
- Child lessons should use their own primary instruments rather than accumulating on the unit page.

## Definition of done

The unit is successful when the four children feel like distinct steps in one design process and the learner can explain why walls are drawn after the relationships are understood, not before.
