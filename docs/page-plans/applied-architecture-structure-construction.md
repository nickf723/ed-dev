# Structure & Construction Unit Plan

## Identity

**Route:** `/applied-science/architecture/structures-construction`  
**Parent:** Architecture  
**Page type:** Unit

## Learning contract

**Learner goal:** understand structural organization as a coordinated architectural system that carries forces through a buildable physical assembly.

**Primary mental model:** loads need continuous paths through floors, beams/walls, columns/cores, foundations, and ground. Architectural space, grids, openings, envelope, and services must coexist with those paths.

**Evidence of understanding:** given a simplified building, the learner can trace a plausible load path, identify where structural organization affects spatial planning, and explain why a connection or construction sequence can matter even when the final form looks unchanged.

## Scope boundary

This unit teaches architectural structural literacy, not structural engineering design.

Belongs here:

- gravity and lateral force as architectural concepts
- load-path continuity
- stability and support
- structural systems, grids, bays, spans, and cores
- interfaces and joints
- tolerances and constructability
- construction dependencies and sequence

Does not belong here:

- beam/column sizing
- reinforcement design
- connection engineering
- foundation design calculations
- structural-code compliance
- construction safety instruction or contractor means-and-methods

## Atomic lesson sequence

1. **Loads & Structural Logic** — force → path → support → ground
2. **Structural Systems & Grids** — choose an organization for those paths
3. **Assemblies & Connections** — make components meet physically
4. **Constructability & Sequence** — make the assembly possible to build over time

## Unit representation

Primary representation: one simplified building skeleton viewed at multiple scales.

The unit should progressively highlight:

- load arrows
- floor/roof collectors
- vertical supports / cores
- grid and bay organization
- key junctions
- construction-order dependencies

The same building should persist across the unit page so the four lessons feel like four questions about one physical object.

## Cross-disciplinary boundary

**Civil / Structural Engineering** asks how structural systems perform quantitatively and how members/connections are designed.  
**Architecture** asks how structural logic coordinates with space, form, envelope, services, circulation, and constructability.

The unit should link to Civil Engineering rather than reproducing its calculations.

## Visual identity

Keep the Architecture blueprint ancestry, but shift from room/site diagrams toward:

- load arrows
- structural skeletons
- bay grids
- exploded junctions
- erection/dependency diagrams

Avoid fake structural-analysis software UI, utilization percentages, stress colors, or safety scores unless backed by a real calculation model.

## Definition of done

The unit is ready when the learner can explain:

> A structure is not hidden magic inside architecture. It is a continuous organization of forces, components, connections, and construction decisions that shares the same geometry as the spaces people use.
