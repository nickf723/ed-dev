# Human Scale & Accessibility Lesson Plan

## Identity

**Page:** Human Scale & Accessibility

**Parent:** Spatial Design & Program  
**Page type:** Atomic lesson

## Learning contract

**Learner goal**  
Understand that architectural dimensions must accommodate user/device envelopes, clearances, tasks, and maneuvering rather than one imaginary average body.

**Prior knowledge**  
Program & Area, Adjacency & Zoning, and Circulation & Wayfinding. The learner should already understand what spaces exist, how they relate, and how routes connect them.

**Primary mental model**  
Spatial fit is a containment relationship: the required body/device/task/movement envelope must fit inside the available geometry.

**Evidence of understanding**  
The learner can change a user envelope, desired side clearance, passage width, turning envelope, or turning zone and predict whether the illustrative geometric fit succeeds or fails.

**Likely misconception**  
Accessibility can be reduced to one universal doorway or turning dimension, or designing for an average body is enough.

## Interaction contract

**Primary instrument:** Fit Studio with two coordinated views of the same principle:

1. straight-passage fit
2. turning-envelope fit

Learner controls:

- user/device envelope width
- desired side clearance on each side
- available passage/opening width
- turning envelope diameter
- available turning-zone diameter

The page computes geometric fit only. It must never label the result code compliant or accessible in a legal/professional sense.

Three illustrative presets demonstrate how the same space can pass for one assumed movement envelope and fail for another. Presets are teaching examples, not standards.

## Scope

**Belongs on this page**

- variable human/device envelopes
- passage and maneuvering clearances
- turning as a different spatial requirement from forward passage
- inclusive design as broader than one default body
- the distinction between geometric fit, code compliance, and inclusive usability

**Does not belong on this page**

- jurisdiction-specific accessibility dimensions
- detailed door maneuvering-clearance tables
- restroom fixture standards
- ramp/slope calculations
- legal compliance advice
- a claim that physical clearance covers all accessibility needs

## Reference ideas

- Human scale is plural.
- Accessibility also includes sensory, cognitive, communication, fatigue, and environmental considerations.
- Code minimums are enforceable baselines, not the complete goal of inclusive design.

## Accuracy constraints

All dimensions are labeled illustrative and adjustable. The page explicitly states that real requirements depend on jurisdiction, building type, route condition, fixtures, doors, slopes, reach ranges, and other conditions. Do not add current code numbers without a sourced, jurisdiction-specific future reference workflow.

## Definition of done

- the learner sees passage and turning as related applications of one containment model
- every control has an immediate visible consequence
- pass/fail language is explicitly geometric, not compliance-based
- presets do not look canonical
- accessibility is not reduced to mobility-device clearance alone
- legitimate slider extremes do not clip or overlap
