# Applied Architecture Page Plan

## Identity

**Page:** Architecture  
**Route:** `/applied-science/architecture`  
**Parent:** Applied Sciences  
**Page type:** Branch hub

**Why this page exists separately:** Architecture coordinates human use, site, structure, enclosure, building systems, and project delivery into habitable built environments. It overlaps engineering, materials science, and visual art, but has a distinct integrative design responsibility.

## Learning contract

**Learner goal**  
Understand architecture as the coordination of multiple constraints acting on the same building, and recognize the major branches used to study that coordination.

**Prior knowledge**  
No specialized prerequisite is required at hub level. Familiarity with buildings, rooms, weather, materials, and basic spatial reasoning is sufficient.

**Primary mental model**  
A building is not a single object-design problem. It is a shared geometry where program, site, structure, envelope, technical systems, and delivery constraints must coexist.

**Evidence of understanding**  
A learner can look at a design change such as moving a wall, changing the site, or altering the facade and identify several other architectural systems that may be affected.

**Likely misconception**  
Architecture is primarily visual styling or drawing attractive buildings. The page should make clear that aesthetics matter, but applied architectural design also coordinates performance, safety, use, construction, and environmental constraints.

## Scope

**Belongs on this page**

- architecture as coordinated constraint-solving
- the six major applied-architecture branches
- a building-section model showing those branches occupying the same geometry
- boundaries with civil engineering, materials science, and architecture as visual art
- planned child structure

**Does not belong on this page**

- a survey of architectural styles or famous buildings
- detailed structural calculations
- detailed HVAC, electrical, or plumbing design
- full building-code instruction
- a large interactive drafting/CAD simulator
- the Vitruvian triad as the master ontology of the field

**Planned child lessons / deeper pages**

1. Spatial Design & Program
2. Site & Context
3. Structure & Construction
4. Envelope & Building Science
5. Building Systems
6. Practice, Codes & Delivery

## Knowledge structure

**Ontological organizing principle:** coordinated building systems and design constraints.

**Relationship to parent:** Applied Sciences asks how knowledge becomes practical outcomes. Architecture specializes this by turning many kinds of knowledge into habitable space.

**Relationship to siblings:**

- Civil Engineering emphasizes structural/infrastructure performance.
- Materials Science emphasizes material structure and properties.
- Industrial Design emphasizes products and interfaces.
- Architecture integrates building-scale space, site, enclosure, structure, systems, and delivery.

**Cross-domain distinction:** Humanities → Visual Arts → Architecture studies style, form, history, masterworks, and aesthetic interpretation rather than professional building coordination.

## Chunk plan

1. **Orient:** What does architecture actually coordinate?
2. **Model:** Show one building section containing all six architectural systems.
3. **Organize:** Present the six planned branches and the question each branch asks.
4. **Generalize:** Demonstrate that changing one architectural decision affects several others.
5. **Contrast:** Distinguish applied architecture from neighboring technical and artistic lenses.
6. **Connect:** Return to Applied Sciences and later descend into child branches as they become live.

## Representations

**Primary representation:** coordinated building section.

The section is appropriate because multiple architectural systems literally occupy and compete for the same physical space. It makes coordination visible without inventing a metaphor.

**Secondary representation:** six branch cards, each phrased as a learner/design question.

**Invariant across representations:** the same building is being examined through different architectural responsibilities.

## Interaction contract

The first hub pass does not require a large interactive instrument. Navigation and ontology are primary.

Planned child nodes remain visibly disabled until real lessons exist. Do not turn planned branches into fake links merely to make the hub feel interactive.

A future lightweight interaction may highlight building-section layers if it improves comprehension without weakening navigation semantics.

## Accuracy check

- Architecture is not reduced to aesthetics.
- Structure and construction are distinguished from architecture's coordinating role.
- Building science includes heat, air, moisture, weather, daylight, and enclosure behavior.
- Building systems include mechanical, electrical, plumbing, lighting, acoustics, and life safety without pretending architects alone engineer each system.
- Codes/accessibility/delivery are presented as constraints and coordination responsibilities, not as a complete professional-practice curriculum.
- The Visual Arts architecture cross-link is explicitly aesthetic/historical in scope.

## Definition of done

The hub is ready when:

- the learner can explain architecture as coordinated constraint-solving

- planned children are visibly planned and not clickable
- the building-section model clarifies the ontology
- the page feels like Applied Sciences while retaining a strong blueprint identity
- the neighboring-field distinctions are clear
- the page passes rendered desktop and narrower-width verification
- no content clips or relies on microtext for essential meaning
