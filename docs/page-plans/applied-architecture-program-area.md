# Program & Area Lesson Plan

## Identity

**Page:** Program & Area  
**Route:** `/applied-science/architecture/spatial-design/program-area`  
**Parent:** Spatial Design & Program  
**Page type:** Atomic lesson

## Learning contract

**Learner goal**  
Understand how an architectural program converts activities, capacity, furniture/equipment, and movement assumptions into a provisional area budget before a floor plan fixes room geometry.

**Prior knowledge**  
The learner should understand that Spatial Design begins with activities and relationships before walls.

**Primary mental model**  
Area is a budget assembled from human use and spatial allowances. A program establishes what must fit; it does not determine the final room shape.

**Evidence of understanding**  
The learner can predict how changing capacity, per-user allowance, furniture, clearance, or grossing assumptions changes net and rough gross planning area.

**Likely misconception**  
A programmed area is a precise required room size or a floor-plan dimension. The lesson must keep area targets visibly provisional and separate area from geometry.

## Scope

**Belongs on this page**

- activities and capacity as program inputs
- furniture/equipment footprint
- in-room circulation and clearance allowance
- net programmed area
- rough grossing allowance as an early planning concept
- distinction between area and room shape
- adjustable assumptions and cause/effect

**Does not belong on this page**

- adjacency planning
- circulation networks between rooms
- code occupancy-load calculations
- accessibility standards and dimensions
- detailed cost estimating
- final floor-plan design

## Chunk plan

1. **Orient:** A program estimates what must fit before deciding exactly how it fits.
2. **Model:** Break one reading-room area budget into activity, contents, clearance, and grossing components.
3. **Manipulate:** Adjust each planning assumption independently.
4. **Test:** Observe which budget components change and which remain fixed.
5. **Generalize:** Net area and rough gross area answer different planning questions.
6. **Contrast:** The same area can produce many room shapes.
7. **Connect:** Adjacency & Zoning will decide where programmed spaces belong relative to one another.

## Representations

**Primary representation:** interactive area-budget composition.

**Secondary representation:** square area diagram used only as a scale reference.

**Invariant:** the programmed area remains an area quantity regardless of the eventual room proportions.

## Interaction contract

**Primary instrument:** Area Budget Studio.

**Controls:**

- users
- activity allowance per user
- furniture/equipment footprint
- in-room clearance percentage
- rough grossing percentage

**Meaningful defaults:** a balanced hypothetical library reading room.

**Curated examples:** compact, balanced, and generous reading-room assumptions.

**Cause and effect:** every control updates the component budget, total net programmed area, rough gross area, and scale diagram immediately.

The lesson must state that all numerical assumptions are illustrative and adjustable, not universal code requirements.

## Reference material

Keep three compact distinctions near the instrument:

- program is not plan
- net is not gross
- early precision is provisional

## Visual identity

Use the Architecture blueprint environment, but let the atomic lesson focus on an area-composition diagram rather than another building section. The page should feel more precise and instrument-like than the hub while remaining readable and non-CAD-like.

## Accuracy constraints

- Do not present an illustrative m²-per-user value as a universal architectural standard.
- Do not equate room-level net programmed area with total building gross area.
- Do not imply that grossing percentages are fixed or code-prescribed.
- Do not imply that a square visualization is the intended room shape.
- Do not use code occupancy factors unless a future lesson explicitly teaches them with jurisdiction/source context.

## Definition of done

The lesson is ready when:

- one central interactive area budget dominates the page
- each control has a visible causal consequence
- illustrative assumptions are clearly labeled
- the learner can distinguish program, net area, rough gross area, and geometry
- no legitimate control state clips or overlaps
- the page has a clear parent link and a non-clickable planned next lesson
