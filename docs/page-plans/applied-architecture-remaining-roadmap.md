# Remaining Applied Architecture Roadmap

This document defines the planned structure beneath the four Architecture branches that remain non-live after Spatial Design & Program and Site & Context.

The purpose is to settle lesson boundaries before any additional visual production begins.

## Global rule

Applied Architecture should teach enough technical logic to coordinate buildings intelligently without quietly becoming a duplicate of Civil Engineering, Mechanical Engineering, Electrical Engineering, Materials Science, construction law, or professional licensure training.

Each unit therefore asks an architectural coordination question:

- **Structure & Construction:** How do forces find a physical path through a buildable assembly?
- **Envelope & Building Science:** How does the enclosure continuously control heat, air, moisture, weather, and light?
- **Building Systems:** How do services reach occupied spaces, return or discharge, and coexist with architecture and structure?
- **Practice, Codes & Delivery:** How do requirements, information, participants, cost, contracts, and construction turn design intent into a delivered building?

All children below remain planned until their pages are genuinely built and verified.

## Structure & Construction

**Unit throughline:** force → load path → structural system → connection → construction sequence

Planned atomic lessons:

1. **Loads & Structural Logic**
   - gravity and lateral force as architectural concepts
   - load paths to foundations/ground
   - stability versus mere strength
   - primary model: trace a force through a simplified building skeleton
   - boundary: no member sizing or structural-design calculations

2. **Structural Systems & Grids**
   - frames, bearing walls, slabs, cores, bays, spans, and grids
   - how structural organization affects room planning, openings, facade rhythm, and service zones
   - primary model: alter a bay/grid and observe spatial coordination consequences
   - boundary: no claim that one system is universally optimal

3. **Assemblies & Connections**
   - components only become buildings when interfaces are resolved
   - bearing, fastening, joints, movement, tolerances, transitions, and constructible edges
   - primary model: zoom from whole-building skeleton into a coordinated junction
   - boundary: connection engineering remains an engineering responsibility where applicable

4. **Constructability & Sequence**
   - permanent design depends on temporary order of operations
   - access, staging, tolerances, prefabrication, temporary support, weather exposure, and dependency
   - primary model: dependency/sequence map for a simplified assembly
   - boundary: educational sequencing model, not construction means-and-methods or safety instruction

## Envelope & Building Science

**Unit throughline:** exterior condition → control layer → continuity → interior condition

The central mental model is a continuous enclosure around conditioned space. Individual materials matter, but failures often occur where a control function is interrupted at corners, roofs, foundations, penetrations, and openings.

Planned atomic lessons:

1. **Heat & Thermal Flow**
   - heat moves through assemblies when temperature conditions differ
   - insulation and thermal bridges change the path/resistance
   - primary model: layered section with an optional conductive bridge
   - boundary: conceptual/illustrative unless real material properties and boundary conditions are supplied

2. **Air & Moisture Control**
   - air leakage can transport heat and moisture
   - vapor and condensation risk depend on temperature, humidity, materials, climate, and assembly
   - primary model: trace air and moisture pathways through a wall section
   - boundary: no universal vapor-barrier prescription or compliance verdict

3. **Weather, Drainage & Durability**
   - bulk water should be shed, drained, flashed, and directed outward
   - drying, exposure, material compatibility, and maintenance affect durability
   - primary model: follow rain through a layered wall/window junction and repair broken drainage continuity
   - boundary: no product-specific warranty or jurisdiction-specific detailing standard

4. **Openings, Daylight & Solar Control**
   - windows interrupt opaque-envelope continuity while admitting view and light
   - size, orientation, glazing, shading, privacy, glare, and solar exposure trade off
   - primary model: adjustable opening + shading geometry with qualitative daylight/exposure response
   - boundary: no photometric, energy, or code claim without real inputs

## Building Systems

**Unit throughline:** source → distribution → terminal/use → return/disposal → access for maintenance

The unit page should emphasize that services occupy real space. Shafts, ceilings, equipment rooms, risers, clearances, access panels, and crossings must be coordinated with structure, envelope, acoustics, and program.

Planned atomic lessons:

1. **HVAC & Thermal Comfort**
   - heating/cooling/ventilation as environmental services
   - supply, return, zones, air movement, equipment, and controls
   - primary model: simplified zone/air loop
   - boundary: no equipment sizing or mechanical design calculation

2. **Water & Plumbing**
   - potable supply, fixtures, drainage, venting, hot water, and vertical stacking
   - gravity drainage behaves differently from pressurized supply
   - primary model: trace supply and waste paths through a simplified section
   - boundary: no pipe sizing or plumbing-code compliance

3. **Power, Lighting & Controls**
   - source/panel, distribution, circuits, loads, switching, sensors, and control zones
   - lighting is both electrical infrastructure and spatial/environmental design
   - primary model: circuit/control diagram linked to spaces
   - boundary: no electrical load calculation or wiring instruction

4. **Acoustics & Sound Control**
   - source → transmission path → receiver
   - absorption, isolation, background noise, room geometry, and system noise affect experience
   - primary model: trace direct/flanking paths and change qualitative control strategies
   - boundary: no calibrated acoustical prediction without real assemblies and measurements

5. **Fire & Life-Safety Systems**
   - detection, alarm, suppression, compartmentation interfaces, smoke control where applicable, emergency power, and egress coordination
   - primary model: coordinated life-safety layer over a simplified plan/section
   - boundary: never present generic dimensions or system rules as jurisdiction-independent code compliance

## Practice, Codes & Delivery

**Unit throughline:** requirement → design information → coordination → commitment → construction → verification/change

This branch should explain professional/project process without pretending to provide legal advice, licensure preparation, or one jurisdiction's mandated workflow.

Planned atomic lessons:

1. **Regulatory Frameworks & Approvals**
   - distinguish zoning/planning controls, building codes, accessibility rules, fire requirements, permits, and other project-specific authorities
   - primary model: requirement map showing different authorities acting on the same design
   - boundary: always jurisdiction-dependent; no legal or compliance verdict

2. **Documentation & Drawing Sets**
   - plans, sections, elevations, details, schedules, specifications, and cross-references describe one coordinated building at different scales
   - primary model: click one building condition and follow it across several document views
   - boundary: no claim that one sheet organization is universally standard

3. **Consultants & Coordination**
   - architecture, structure, civil, MEP, landscape, specialist systems, owner requirements, and contractor input must share geometry and decisions
   - primary model: coordination issue board / clash-resolution chain
   - boundary: coordination responsibilities vary by contract and project organization

4. **Cost & Schedule**
   - scope, quantity, quality, uncertainty, phasing, lead time, and design decisions affect cost and time
   - primary model: change a few project assumptions and see qualitative cost/schedule pressures rather than a fake price oracle
   - boundary: no real estimate without project/location/market data

5. **Procurement & Delivery Methods**
   - compare common ways participants, contracts, design, and construction can be organized
   - primary model: role/information relationship diagrams, not a winner ranking
   - boundary: terminology and legal implications vary by jurisdiction and contract

6. **Construction Administration & Closeout**
   - submittals, RFIs, observations, changes, payment review, punch/deficiency tracking, record information, commissioning/closeout interfaces
   - primary model: issue → clarification/review → field resolution information loop
   - boundary: no legal advice and no substitution for project contracts or professional obligations

## Architecture branch sequence

The six top-level Architecture units now have a deliberate progression:

1. **Spatial Design & Program** — organize human use
2. **Site & Context** — respond to place
3. **Structure & Construction** — make the physical assembly stand and be buildable
4. **Envelope & Building Science** — mediate exterior and interior environments
5. **Building Systems** — distribute services through the building
6. **Practice, Codes & Delivery** — coordinate requirements and deliver the project

This is an organizing sequence, not a claim that real architectural design proceeds linearly. In practice these systems are iterative and tightly coupled.
