# Envelope & Building Science Unit Plan

## Identity

**Route:** `/applied-science/architecture/building-science`  
**Parent:** Architecture  
**Page type:** Unit

## Learning contract

**Learner goal:** understand the building enclosure as a continuous environmental control system rather than a decorative facade or stack of unrelated materials.

**Primary mental model:** exterior conditions act on a continuous enclosure. Different layers and details control heat, air, moisture, bulk water, light, and view while protecting an interior environment.

**Evidence of understanding:** the learner can trace one control function around a wall/roof/foundation/opening transition and identify why a small discontinuity can matter more than a large area of otherwise good material.

## Scope boundary

Belongs here:

- heat-flow paths and thermal bridges
- insulation continuity
- air leakage and air-control continuity
- moisture pathways and condensation risk as a coupled phenomenon
- bulk-water shedding, flashing, drainage, and drying
- openings as interruptions in opaque assemblies
- daylight, solar exposure, glare, privacy, and shading as enclosure concerns

Does not belong here:

- manufacturer-specific product specification
- universal vapor-barrier prescriptions
- full hygrothermal simulation
- calibrated energy modeling
- code compliance without real climate, assembly, and jurisdiction inputs
- material chemistry that belongs in Materials Science

## Atomic lesson sequence

1. **Heat & Thermal Flow** — temperature difference → path → resistance / bridge
2. **Air & Moisture Control** — pressure/humidity conditions → leakage / vapor pathways → risk
3. **Weather, Drainage & Durability** — rain exposure → shedding / flashing / drainage / drying
4. **Openings, Daylight & Solar Control** — interrupt the opaque enclosure while balancing light, view, exposure, shading, and continuity

## Unit representation

Primary representation: one continuous building-section loop from foundation to wall to window to roof.

The learner should be able to switch the highlighted control function while the geometry remains fixed. The important invariant is continuity around corners, penetrations, and openings.

## Cross-disciplinary boundary

**Materials Science** explains material properties.  
**Mechanical Engineering** may model loads and environmental systems quantitatively.  
**Architecture / Building Science** coordinates how assemblies and details combine those properties into a continuous enclosure.

## Visual identity

Use sectional cuts, layer stacks, continuity lines, thermal bridges, flashing paths, and opening details. Avoid fake energy dashboards or red/green performance grades unless driven by an explicit quantitative model.

## Definition of done

The unit is ready when the learner can explain:

> A high-performing envelope is not one magic material. It is a set of control functions that must remain continuous across the entire boundary between inside and outside.
