# Access & Surroundings Page Plan

## Identity

**Route:** `/applied-science/architecture/site-context/access-surroundings`  
**Parent:** Site & Context  
**Page type:** Atomic lesson

## Learning contract

**Learner goal:** understand site edges as relationships with movement, service, neighbors, landscape, views, noise, and public space rather than as boundaries where design thinking stops.

**Primary mental model:** each site edge connects the project to a different surrounding condition. Public arrival, service access, outlook, and disturbance may reinforce or conflict with one another depending on where they meet the site.

**Evidence of understanding:** the learner can move independent site intentions to different edges and identify the tradeoffs created when they coincide or separate.

## Scope

Belongs here:

- public/pedestrian arrival
- service/back-of-house access
- neighboring-building relationships
- street and public-realm relationship
- landscape edge
- preferred outlook
- illustrative noise/disturbance direction
- tradeoffs when several intentions use the same edge

Does not belong here:

- traffic engineering calculations
- code-specific driveway or fire-access requirements
- final parking layout
- acoustic calculations
- real property access rights
- a universal scoring system for site edges

## Primary instrument

A fixed hypothetical four-edge site context:

- North: neighboring building
- East: service lane
- South: public street
- West: landscape edge

The learner independently assigns:

1. public arrival edge
2. service access edge
3. preferred outlook
4. illustrative noise source

The relationship panel reports notable coincidences, including shared public/service edge, outlook/noise conflict, arrival/outlook alignment, and service/neighbor adjacency.

## Guardrails

- no relationship state is automatically good or bad
- no combined score is shown
- fixed surroundings are hypothetical and exist only to teach edge relationships
- actual access depends on survey, ownership, jurisdiction, transportation context, accessibility, operations, emergency/service needs, utilities, and project requirements
- views and noise can change over time and depend on more than compass direction
