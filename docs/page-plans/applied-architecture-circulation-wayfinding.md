# Circulation & Wayfinding Lesson Plan

## Identity

**Page:** Circulation & Wayfinding  
**Route:** `/applied-science/architecture/spatial-design/circulation-wayfinding`  
**Parent:** Spatial Design & Program  
**Page type:** Atomic lesson

## Learning contract

**Learner goal**  
Understand circulation as a network of available connections and wayfinding as the problem of making that network understandable at moments of choice.

**Prior knowledge**  
Program & Area and Adjacency & Zoning. The learner should know which spaces exist and which relationships are desirable before designing paths among them.

**Primary mental model**  
Movement is a graph: spaces are nodes, passages are edges, and a route is a connected sequence from origin to destination. Wayfinding quality depends on how clearly people can interpret that sequence and its decision points.

**Evidence of understanding**  
The learner can open or close a path connection, predict whether a route becomes shorter, longer, or disconnected, and identify intermediate decision points that may need stronger spatial cues.

**Likely misconception**  
The shortest route is always the best route, more connections are always better, or circulation is just unused leftover area between rooms.

## Interaction contract

**Primary instrument:** editable circulation network for the same small library used in the earlier lessons.

The learner can:

- choose a destination from the entry
- enable or disable individual connections
- compare direct-spine, loop, and sparse presets
- see the current shortest available route highlighted
- see approximate route length and intermediate decision count
- intentionally disconnect a destination and observe the boundary case

The network uses abstract plan units rather than pretending to teach real corridor lengths.

## Wayfinding scope

The instrument tracks connection, approximate route length, and intermediate decisions only. The page explicitly states that real wayfinding also depends on sightlines, landmarks, spatial hierarchy, signs, lighting, familiarity, crowding, accessibility, and other cues.

No egress-code widths, travel-distance limits, or jurisdiction-specific requirements belong on this page.

## Reference rules

- Movement consumes programmed space.
- More connections add flexibility but can add complexity and conflict.
- A slightly longer route can be easier to understand than a short but ambiguous one.
- Decision points need stronger environmental information than uninterrupted path segments.

## Definition of done

- every connection toggle produces an immediate route consequence
- disconnected states are handled clearly
- route highlighting never implies the computed path is the only valid architectural route
- the graph remains legible at narrower widths
- wayfinding is not reduced to shortest-path mathematics
- code-specific circulation rules are deferred to appropriate future content
