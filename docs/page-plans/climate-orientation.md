# Climate & Orientation Page Plan

## Identity

**Route:** `/applied-science/architecture/site-context/climate-orientation`  
**Parent:** Site & Context  
**Page type:** Atomic lesson

## Learning contract

**Learner goal:** understand how building orientation changes geometric exposure to directional environmental forces without confusing exposure with performance.

**Primary mental model:** each building face has an outward bearing. A directional source such as an illustrative sun or wind bearing meets some faces more directly than others. Rotating the building changes those relationships.

**Evidence of understanding:** the learner can rotate a building or source bearing and predict which face becomes more directly exposed.

## Scope

Belongs here:

- compass bearings
- building long-axis orientation
- façade outward directions
- geometric facing relationship to illustrative sun and wind bearings
- the distinction between exposure and architectural response

Does not belong here:

- universal claims about an optimal orientation
- a full sun-path simulator
- energy, daylight, comfort, wind-load, or ventilation calculations
- climate-zone prescriptions
- real-time weather

## Primary instrument

An orientation plan with three independent controls:

1. building long-axis bearing
2. illustrative sun bearing
3. illustrative wind bearing

Four building faces update together. Each face reports a geometric facing percentage for the two sources.

The percentage is only a cosine-based alignment measure. It must not be presented as heat gain, pressure, energy performance, daylight, or comfort.

## Guardrails

- actual solar position depends on location, date, time, horizon, and obstruction
- wind is variable and statistical rather than one permanent arrow
- orientation decisions also depend on access, views, noise, program, structure, envelope, landscape, and urban context
- avoid directional folklore such as “south is always best”
