# Building Systems Unit Plan

## Identity

**Route:** `/applied-science/architecture/building-systems`  
**Parent:** Architecture  
**Page type:** Unit

## Learning contract

**Learner goal:** understand building services as spatial networks that require sources, distribution routes, terminals, return/disposal paths, controls, and maintenance access.

**Primary mental model:** systems occupy the same building volume as rooms, structure, envelope, and circulation. A service cannot simply be drawn as a symbol at its destination; it needs a coordinated path through the building.

**Evidence of understanding:** the learner can trace a simplified service from source to use and explain where shafts, ceilings, equipment rooms, risers, access, noise, structure, and enclosure may affect the route.

## Scope boundary

Belongs here:

- architectural coordination of HVAC, plumbing, electrical, lighting, acoustics, and life-safety systems
- sources, distribution, terminals, return/disposal, controls, and access
- service zones, risers, shafts, ceilings, equipment rooms, and crossings
- how systems affect program, ceiling height, structure, envelope, noise, and maintenance

Does not belong here:

- equipment selection/sizing
- pipe/duct/conductor sizing
- wiring or installation instruction
- engineered fire-protection design
- calibrated acoustic prediction
- code-compliance declarations from generic examples

## Atomic lesson sequence

1. **HVAC & Thermal Comfort** — condition/ventilate space through supply, return, zones, equipment, and controls
2. **Water & Plumbing** — move supply water to use and waste water away, with vertical stacking and gravity/pressure differences
3. **Power, Lighting & Controls** — distribute power and organize loads, lighting, switching, sensing, and control zones
4. **Acoustics & Sound Control** — source → transmission path → receiver, with absorption, isolation, room behavior, and system noise
5. **Fire & Life-Safety Systems** — coordinate detection, notification, suppression, compartment interfaces, emergency systems, and egress-related layers

## Unit representation

Primary representation: one building section with a selectable service network.

For every service, use the same grammar:

**source → distribution → terminal/use → return/disposal → maintenance access**

Keeping the section fixed should reveal competition for the same ceilings, shafts, rooms, and penetrations.

## Coordination lesson

The unit page itself should teach coordination rather than becoming a sixth generic dashboard. Example questions:

- What happens when a duct wants to cross a beam?
- What happens when plumbing fixtures are scattered vertically?
- What happens when equipment needs replacement access?
- What happens when a service penetration crosses an envelope or rated assembly?
- What happens when systems create noise in quiet program spaces?

## Cross-disciplinary boundary

Mechanical, electrical, plumbing, fire-protection, and acoustical specialists may design systems quantitatively. Architecture must understand enough of the network and spatial consequences to reserve space, coordinate interfaces, and preserve design intent.

## Definition of done

The unit is ready when the learner can explain:

> Building systems are not invisible utilities added after architecture. They are networks with physical routes, equipment, terminals, controls, and access requirements that must be coordinated from the beginning.
