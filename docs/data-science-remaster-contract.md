# Data Science Remaster Contract

## Identity and rotation role

Data Science is a Formal Science navigation hub and an evidence-audit lesson. It follows a Visual Arts pass to change academic world, visual grammar, interaction, and source boundary. The page should feel like a transparent analytical workbench: records become features, features enter a model, and every result crosses an evaluation boundary before it can support a claim.

The page must not reduce data science to machine learning, imply that an optimized partition is a natural category system, or treat a high score as sufficient evidence of validity.

## Bounded direct-child ontology

Data Science owns eight direct branches:

1. Data Collection & Wrangling
2. Exploratory Data Analysis
3. Statistical Modeling & Inference
4. Machine Learning
5. Data Engineering
6. Visualization & Communication
7. Experiments & Causal Inference
8. Responsible Data Science & Evaluation

All eight are planned, non-clickable units until substantive pages exist. Algorithms, model families, chart types, databases, causal designs, metrics, and industry applications belong inside these branches before another direct peer is proposed.

The eight-way practice map is the first major section after the page header. It presents the branches as recurring kinds of work rather than a rigid waterfall.

## Overview lesson flow

The root follows a Navigate → Model → Evaluate → Distinguish → Check sequence:

1. navigate the eight registry-owned branches and a six-question analytical stack;
2. model one transparent unsupervised procedure through assign and recenter steps;
3. inspect provenance, leakage, baselines, held-out evidence, shift, and decision cost;
4. distinguish description, prediction, and causation;
5. assess exact metric arithmetic and three evidence boundaries.

The root is an overview. It does not attempt to teach every algorithm, replace Statistics, prescribe one software stack, or offer domain-independent rules for responsible deployment.

## Deterministic model contract

One shared module owns branch parity, a fixed 72-point sample, four initial centroids, K-means transitions, classification-metric arithmetic, and assessment fixtures. The K-means laboratory offers `k = 2`, `3`, or `4` and exposes:

- nearest-centroid assignment under squared Euclidean distance;
- centroid recentering as the mean of assigned points;
- full assign-and-recenter iterations;
- completed-iteration count;
- within-cluster sum of squared distances.

The default sample contains three compact spatial clouds favorable to this objective. Results still depend on `k`, feature representation and scale, initialization, outliers, and geometry. The partition is a fitted model, not ground truth.

The server-rendered background reuses the same fixed coordinates to show the page's records → features → model progression without viewport measurement, random geometry, canvas, or a client-only first frame.

## Assessment contract

The evidence lab contains four deterministic files:

- calculate 90% accuracy and approximately 95.5% recall from a 42/2/48/8 confusion matrix;
- exclude a field created after the prediction-time boundary;
- recognize that repeated test-set selection turns the test set into tuning evidence;
- describe an observational association without silently claiming an intervention effect.

Every file supports unanswered, correct, incorrect, changed-answer, switched-file, and reset states. Feedback reconstructs the denominator or evidence boundary rather than merely announcing a score.

## Vocabulary ownership

Data Science currently owns eighteen root terms spanning datasets, observations, features, targets, sampling, missingness, provenance, evaluation splits, leakage, generalization, overfitting, clustering, K-means, calibration, distribution shift, and confounding.

The terms register at `formal.data-science` and aggregate into the Formal Science drawer through curriculum containment. As child units are built, narrower terms should move to their owning child registrations while remaining inherited by this parent.

## Source and model boundary

K-means behavior and limitations are checked against the official scikit-learn clustering guide. Leakage and test-set boundaries are checked against scikit-learn's official common-pitfalls guide. The responsible-evaluation horizon links to the NIST AI Risk Management Framework.

The page's sample, confusion matrix, hospital timeline, train/validation/test loop, and causal diagram are local schematic teaching fixtures. They are not empirical datasets or case-study results.
