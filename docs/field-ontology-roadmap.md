# Field Ontology Roadmap

Education Station 64 is organized by the shape of knowledge, not by a quota. The number **64 remains part of the brand**, but it does not require 64 first-order fields.

The five canonical branches are:

- Formal Science
- Natural Science
- Social Science
- Humanities
- Applied Science

Interdisciplines is a relational atlas over those branches. It reveals meaningful overlaps and links back to canonical owners rather than storing duplicate curricula.

## First-order field admission test

A subject becomes a first-order field only when it can answer all of these questions convincingly:

1. Does it have central questions distinct from its proposed siblings?
2. Does it have recognizable methods, evidence, practices, or representations of its own?
3. Can it sustain a meaningful child curriculum rather than one overview page?
4. Would first-order placement make the ontology easier to navigate?
5. Can we build a substantive parent page and at least one honest next step without manufacturing placeholder routes?

If not, it belongs as a child, lens, cross-link, or planned node under an existing field.

## Strong first-order candidates

The most promising additions are concentrated in Social and Applied Science.

### Social Science

- **Archaeology** — may be promoted from Anthropology when its material evidence, field methods, dating, landscape, heritage, and bioarchaeology curriculum justify a dedicated field home.
- **Criminology** — distinct when centered on crime, harm, institutions, measurement, causation, prevention, and justice-system outcomes rather than only criminal law.
- **Demography / Population Studies** — distinct when population measurement, fertility, mortality, migration, age structure, projection, and policy consequences form the organizing method.
- **Public Policy** — distinct when policy design, implementation, evaluation, institutions, tradeoffs, and evidence use form a sustained curriculum.
- **International Relations** — distinct when interstate systems, diplomacy, conflict, cooperation, international institutions, political economy, and global governance form a sustained curriculum.

### Applied Science

- **Veterinary Science** — animal health, comparative medicine, population health, welfare, diagnostics, and clinical practice.
- **Urban & Regional Planning** — land use, transportation, housing, infrastructure, environment, public space, governance, and community process.
- **Manufacturing** — production systems, processes, automation, quality, metrology, supply chains, safety, and continuous improvement.
- **Transportation & Logistics** — movement networks, routing, capacity, infrastructure, freight, transit, reliability, safety, and operations.

These are candidates, not automatic promotions. Each should be activated only with a real page and coherent child structure.

## Promotion process

1. Build or audit the proposed parent and current child route.
2. Write the field's central questions, methods, evidence, and direct-child map.
3. Compare it with neighboring first-order fields for duplication.
4. Decide one canonical owner and mark interdisciplinary relationships as cross-links.
5. Build the substantive field page and vocabulary contribution.
6. Add redirects if an existing route moves.
7. Validate sidebar ancestry, breadcrumbs, vocabulary aggregation, and page context.
8. Commit and push the coherent migration to `main`.
