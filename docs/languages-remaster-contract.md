# Languages Root Remaster Contract

## Page job

`/humanities/languages` is a field hub and compact orientation lesson. Its
primary job is to help a learner distinguish language traditions from the
practices used to learn, interpret, translate, read, write, and participate in
them without treating languages as flags, word lists, or interchangeable codes.

The page should leave one durable model:

> Language ability grows through repeated cycles of noticing, understanding,
> retrieving, using, receiving feedback, and adapting to audience, register,
> genre, modality, and community context.

## Preservation inventory

The remaster preserves:

- the source-and-target translation-desk identity;
- the three-set multilingual phrase window;
- the four fixed English–Spanish translation contrasts;
- the eight existing direct Languages branches;
- the five-part Notice → Understand → Retrieve → Use → Adapt loop;
- the explicit cross-link to Linguistics.

The pass replaces a viewport-measured animated canvas with a deterministic
server-rendered reading desk. It replaces two compressed branch banks around a
fixed central column with full-width peer navigation and separates the two
interactive instruments with scenery corridors. It does not introduce a live
machine translator or claim that a curated phrase table represents a language,
speaker population, or culture.

All eight remain planned and inert because their destination pages do not yet
exist. Two visual folios—languages and language traditions, and practices for
learning and use—are explicitly groupings rather than hidden curriculum
parents. Linguistics remains a neighboring Social Science field: its structural
analysis can support language learning without being duplicated as a Languages
child.

## Learning and assessment contract

The hub proceeds through:

1. registry-owned direct-child navigation;
2. the five-stage practice loop and Linguistics boundary;
3. the preserved multilingual phrase window;
4. the preserved translation-choices studio;
5. six language-learning principles;
6. a deterministic four-case evidence review;
7. official catalog and archive boundaries.

The evidence review tests:

- an exact sample statement: the “Thank you” display has one right-to-left
  entry among seven, so `1 / 7 = 14.3%`;
- communicative translation rather than word-for-word substitution;
- signed languages as distinct natural languages rather than universal gesture
  or manual copies of surrounding spoken languages;
- the difference among a language-catalog record, software-locale data,
  archive metadata, content access, rights, and community knowledge.

Every numerical statement names the displayed sample and denominator. It is
never generalized into a claim about all languages, scripts, writing practices,
or speakers.

## Representation parity

`languagesModel.ts` owns the exact direct-branch order, five practice stages,
three phrase datasets, direction-share arithmetic, four translation examples,
and assessment fixtures. The phrase window consumes the same table used by the
exact assessment; the translation studio and background consume the same fixed
translation examples; the page and background consume the same five stages.

No random number, current time, viewport size, post-hydration drawing, remote
translation, or fake live result participates in the initial render. The route
and background are server-owned; only the phrase, translation, and evidence
islands hydrate.

## Vocabulary ownership

The root owns six concepts taught across the lesson:

- Language Use;
- Communicative Competence;
- Receptive Language Skill;
- Productive Language Skill;
- Register;
- Language-Learning Feedback.

Each direct branch owns two narrower terms, producing 22 unique terms in the
Languages subtree. Curriculum containment aggregates those terms into
Languages and Humanities with source-node provenance.

## Source and future repository boundary

The root performs no render-time provider request. It links three public
interfaces and defines future adapters:

- [Glottolog Downloads](https://glottolog.org/meta/downloads) for versioned
  languoid, classification, geographic, and bibliographic datasets;
- [Unicode CLDR](https://cldr.unicode.org/index/downloads) for versioned locale
  names, formats, collation, and other software-internationalization data;
- [Open Language Archives Community](https://www.language-archives.org/) for
  interoperable metadata from participating language-resource archives.

A future normalized record must retain provider and record identifiers,
release or datestamp, source URL, retrieval time, names, codes, scope,
classification or inheritance context, contributor-supplied fields, access,
rights, license, and pagination or resumption state where applicable. A
cataloged languoid, locale, archive record, media object, transcription,
translation, learning resource, community-preferred name, and permission to use
content remain distinct evidence objects. Provider failure may never be
disguised as a successful live repository.

## Verification contract

Automated checks cover:

- exact eight-branch order and planned status;
- exact five-stage practice sequence;
- all three phrase-table sizes, left-to-right and right-to-left counts, shares,
  and formatted percentages;
- all four translation fixtures;
- four evidence cases and every answer path;
- 22 unique vocabulary terms and direct-child aggregation;
- generated HTML ancestry, planned non-link behavior, model values,
  assessment, source links, vocabulary, and absence of canvas, randomness, or
  animation-loop markers;
- TypeScript, touched-file lint, formatting, repository audits, and production
  build.

Rendered desktop, narrower desktop, mobile, keyboard, zoom, phrase, translation,
and evidence states remain in the visual-verification queue while the workspace
browser/server limitation persists.
