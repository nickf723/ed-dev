# History remaster contract

## Scope

This checkpoint remasters the History root without collapsing the field into a timeline or a collection of famous events. The shared recipe remains the owner of the root navigation and printing-press case study; a custom lesson extension adds source method, chronology arithmetic, assessment, repository boundaries, and branch-owned vocabulary.

## Ontology

- History has exactly three direct organizing lenses: Time, Place, and Theme.
- These lenses overlap. A historical question can require all three without becoming a fourth root branch.
- Time owns chronology and periodization concepts; Place owns scale, borders, migration, networks, and historical geography; Theme owns comparison, causation, contingency, agency, and structure.
- The root owns source method, evidence, provenance, contextualization, corroboration, interpretation, archives, and archival silence.
- Direct children appear before supporting lesson content and derive from the curriculum registry.

## Learning flow

The root proceeds from three direct lenses to a fixed printing-press case study, then to source reading, elapsed-year arithmetic, a four-file evidence assessment, and collection/source boundaries. Learners practice one rote computation and multiple reasoning distinctions: primary versus reliable, sequence versus causation, and periodization versus a universal historical switch.

## Models and boundaries

- The interval instrument converts BCE dates to astronomical year numbering for subtraction, preventing an invented year zero. It reports distance between labeled years, not exact elapsed days.
- Exact chronology can require months, days, calendar systems, uncertainty ranges, and stated inclusive-counting rules.
- A primary source is defined by its relationship to the question and period, not by guaranteed accuracy or neutrality.
- Corroboration can strengthen, limit, or redirect a claim; agreement alone does not remove shared dependence or selection bias.
- Periods are analytical conventions whose usefulness varies with question, scale, region, and process.

## Vocabulary

Thirty-five terms have one narrowest current owner: fourteen at History, nine at Time, six at Place, and six at Theme. Parent drawers aggregate descendant terms through curriculum containment, so future child lessons can extend the same ownership tree without copying root lists.

## Visual and hydration contract

History keeps an archival-editorial grammar: warm paper light, printing marks, dark reading surfaces, source ledgers, and restrained amber/emerald/violet distinctions. Motion is off for the root recipe so the background remains deterministic and stable across server and client render. Client state is limited to the chronology and assessment islands; neither uses random values, viewport measurements, effects, timers, or wall-clock state.

## Source and repository boundary

The page links directly to Library of Congress primary-source guidance, National Archives document-analysis worksheets, the Chronicling America API transition documentation, and Smithsonian Open Access developer tools. A future repository must expose provider, collection scope, item identity, query, pagination, date/place metadata, rights, digitization, and survival bias. These links are source protocols, not a claim that the page already hosts a comprehensive archive.

## Verification

The checkpoint must pass targeted React/Next lint, TypeScript, deterministic history-model tests, vocabulary aggregation, the three informational audits, a production build, generated-HTML checks for direct lenses and initial lesson content, and a zero-deployment guard before publication to `studio`. Responsive rendering and interaction checks remain queued when the browser runner is unavailable.
