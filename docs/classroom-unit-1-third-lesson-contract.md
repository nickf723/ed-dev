# Classroom Unit 1 Third-Lesson Contract

This checkpoint brings each active non-Math Classroom sequence to a coherent early-unit frontier. It adds three different academic worlds without changing the shared Explain → Do → Check lesson grammar.

## Narrator & Perspective

1. **Learner outcome:** Distinguish author, narrator, and perceiving character, then explain how narration controls reader access.
2. **Prerequisite:** Evidence versus inference and numbered-line close reading.
3. **Mental model:** The same event can be presented through different information windows; voice asks who tells, while perspective asks whose inner experience the reader can enter.
4. **Core relationship:** Perspective controls what the reader knows directly, must infer, or cannot know yet.
5. **Primary action:** Switch among original retellings of one scene and inspect the coordinated access ledger.
6. **Misconception:** A first-person narrator is not automatically the author or truthful, and third person is not automatically omniscient.
7. **Transfer:** Classify a fresh excerpt and state precisely whose inner experience is directly available.

**Storyboard:** Compare two unlabeled tellings → name author, narrator, and perspective → shift the information window → track direct, inferred, and withheld access → transfer to a fresh excerpt → practice → conclude.

## Building & Breaking Polymers

1. **Learner outcome:** Trace dehydration synthesis and hydrolysis through changes in bonds, water, chains, and free monomers.
2. **Prerequisite:** Water, CHNOPS, and the general monomer/polymer relationship.
3. **Mental model:** State → reaction move → state; building a bond releases water components, while breaking a bond adds water across it.
4. **Core relationship:** A linear chain of n monomers has n − 1 links; the generic model releases or uses one water molecule per changed link.
5. **Primary action:** Operate a reaction bench and coordinate the visible chain with a bond-and-water ledger.
6. **Misconception:** Water is not glue, dehydration synthesis does not mean merely drying a cell, and not every biological macromolecule is a true polymer.
7. **Transfer:** Determine bond and water counts for a fresh chain or partial hydrolysis case.

**Storyboard:** Notice H and OH ends → form one bond → reverse it with hydrolysis → operate the multi-monomer bench → scale to n − 1 → transfer to partial hydrolysis → practice → conclude.

## Tokugawa Japan & Bourbon France

1. **Learner outcome:** Compare the mechanisms and limitations of centralization in Tokugawa Japan and Bourbon France.
2. **Prerequisite:** Same-lens comparison and the map/control boundary established in the preceding Global II lessons.
3. **Mental model:** Centralization is an institutional network connecting a political center, powerful elites, and regions—not a ruler's unlimited volume knob.
4. **Core relationship:** Both regimes drew elites toward a political center, but alternate attendance/domain order and court/office/provincial systems worked in different contexts.
5. **Primary action:** Follow evidence through mechanism → intended effect → practical limit for each case.
6. **Misconception:** Centralized or absolute does not mean identical, total, or uniform control.
7. **Transfer:** Judge how far attendance, court, administrative, and map evidence can support a comparison claim.

**Storyboard:** Locate the two near-period footprints → model the center-to-region network → compare under one fixed lens → build an institutional chain → test supported and overextended claims → practice → conclude.
