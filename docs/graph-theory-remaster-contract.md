# Graph Theory remaster contract

**Route:** `/formal-science/mathematics/discrete/graph-theory`

**Curriculum node:** `formal.mathematics.discrete.graph-theory`

**Page kind:** bounded root unit

## Learning contract

- **Goal:** A learner can translate a pairwise relationship into vertices and edges, read degree and connectivity from that model, and explain how a traversal discovers routes.
- **Prior knowledge:** finite sets and the idea that a relation can connect two objects.
- **Mental model:** a graph preserves which objects are connected while ignoring irrelevant physical detail; routes are sequences through that relation structure.
- **Evidence:** the learner can calculate degree, trace breadth-first layers, diagnose a disconnected case, and transfer the model to a new situation.
- **Misconception:** a mathematical graph is not necessarily a coordinate plot, and a visually crossing pair of edges is not automatically a new vertex.

## Scope and depth ceiling

The root provides a compact overview of graph modeling, degree, paths, connectivity, and traversal. Six direct planned lessons form the current ceiling:

1. Vertices, Edges & Degree
2. Walks, Trails & Paths
3. Connectivity & Components
4. Trees & Spanning Structure
5. Directed & Weighted Graphs
6. Coloring, Planarity & Networks

Proof-heavy, extremal, spectral, probabilistic, and infinite graph theory remain beyond the current curriculum horizon. They do not become speculative placeholder routes.

## Representation and interaction contract

- One canonical seven-vertex campus network feeds the background, opening worked model, degree readouts, and default traversal.
- The network background is instructional content and must preserve the exact same vertex labels and edges.
- The primary instrument asks one question: how does a traversal reveal reachability and routes?
- Breadth-first and depth-first orders are deterministic. No render-time or reseed randomness is permitted.
- Controls, graph, frontier/visited state, and route interpretation remain co-visible at desktop widths and return to normal document flow on narrow screens.

## Assessment contract

- **Transfer:** reason about whether a route survives a closed connection and name a valid alternative.
- **Fluency:** deterministic small-graph cases ask for a vertex degree or the total degree sum, with solutions calculated from the generated edge list.
- Feedback explains the relationship used; it does not only score the response.

## Visual grammar

The page is a network drafting room: a continuous route rail organizes the unit, the canonical graph becomes a luminous routing board, and scenery corridors expose the same network as large quiet infrastructure. Emerald/cyan indicate discovered structure, amber indicates the active frontier, and violet is reserved for alternate/depth-first structure. The composition must remain structurally different from the Set Theory conservatory and the Discrete Mathematics parent workshop.

## One-sentence success test

This page is better for learning because the learner first sees where Graph Theory goes, then uses one accurate network to translate connection into degree, reachability, and traversal rather than encountering an unlabeled algorithm animation.
