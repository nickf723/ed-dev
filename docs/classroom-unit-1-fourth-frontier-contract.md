# Classroom Unit 1 Fourth-Frontier Contract

This pass advances the next planned lesson in Science, Social Studies, and English. Algebra I Unit 1 is already complete and remains unchanged.

## Carbohydrates

1. **Learner outcome:** Explain how carbohydrate arrangement supports immediate energy use, storage, or structural strength.
2. **Prerequisite:** Elements of Life and Building & Breaking Polymers.
3. **Mental model:** The same monomer family can produce different behavior when linkage and chain geometry change.
4. **Core relationship:** structure → accessible bonds and shape → biological function.
5. **Primary action:** Compare glucose, starch, and cellulose through one coordinated evidence bench.
6. **Misconception:** Carbohydrate does not simply mean sugar, and shared glucose composition does not imply shared function.
7. **Transfer:** Use aligned, hydrogen-bonded chains to distinguish cellulose from starch in a fresh tissue case.

**Storyboard:** Inspect three structures → name arrangement → connect arrangement to consequence → compare functions → reject composition-only reasoning → transfer → conclude.

## Outsiders & Exchange

1. **Learner outcome:** Use policy and geographic evidence to compare controlled channels of exchange.
2. **Prerequisite:** The 1750 baseline and same-lens institutional comparison.
3. **Mental model:** States shaped exchange through gates; restriction and contact can coexist.
4. **Core relationship:** policy rule + enforcement + permitted channel → pattern of exchange.
5. **Primary action:** Compare Tokugawa, Qing, and Atlantic-imperial exchange records.
6. **Misconception:** Restriction is not identical to total isolation, while maritime reach is not evidence of equal or voluntary exchange.
7. **Transfer:** Bound a claim about supervised Dutch trade at Nagasaki.

**Storyboard:** Notice routes and gates → separate rule from outcome → compare channels → name coercive boundaries → test open/closed overclaims → transfer → conclude.

## Character & Desire

1. **Learner outcome:** Infer a character's motive from a pattern of action, speech, attention, and contradiction.
2. **Prerequisite:** Evidence and inference; narrator and perspective.
3. **Mental model:** Desire behaves like a vector under pressure: choices reveal direction even when speech disguises it.
4. **Core relationship:** repeated detail + pressure + choice → defensible motive inference.
5. **Primary action:** Compare three original microfiction cases in a coordinated motive ledger.
6. **Misconception:** A stated wish is evidence, not guaranteed access to the character's true or only motive.
7. **Transfer:** Explain a sealed-letter pattern without inventing an unsupported backstory.

**Storyboard:** Observe a contradiction → gather a pattern → infer a motive → test it against every detail → revise an overclaim → transfer → conclude.
